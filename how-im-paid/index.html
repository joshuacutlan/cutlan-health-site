Pay, explained

Who pays me, and why it isn't you

Fair question: if you never get a bill from me, how do I make a living? Here's the full answer, including the part where it could sway my advice.

Last updated October 21, 2026
In short

You never get a bill from me. When an enrollment comes from me, the carrier pays me a commission, and the premium you pay matches what you'd see buying on your own. If the best answer for you is something I earn nothing on, like staying on your work plan, that's still the answer you'll get.

Where the money comes from

Insurance carriers pay licensed agents to find, enroll and look after their members. That money comes from the carrier's side. A plan carries one price in your state, approved before anyone sells it, and an agent's commission is already inside that number. Skip the agent and the number doesn't drop; use one and it doesn't rise.

Why that matters to you

Because I'm not charging you, you can ask me anything, compare as many options as you want, and walk away if none of them fit. There's nothing to sign and no cost to finding out.

Where my pay could sway my advice

Some plans pay me more than others, and some of the best answers for you pay me nothing. If you're better off keeping your work plan, or your income gets you a big Marketplace subsidy, that's the recommendation. You get the numbers in one table and the reason one option wins, so you can check my math instead of trusting me blindly.

My bet is simple: people who get the real math come back when things change, and they send their friends.

What you get for free

  • Your realistic options priced in one table
  • A rundown of what's covered and what isn't, in normal words
  • Help applying and lining up a start date with no gap
  • Someone who answers when you have a claim question or your life changes

What people ask me

Do I ever get a bill from you?+

No. Nothing is ever billed to you. When an enrollment comes from me, the carrier pays me a commission.

Is my premium higher because I went through you?+

No. A plan carries one price in your state, and that price is the same from the carrier, on the Marketplace, or through me. Agents are paid out of it, never on top of it.

Does that change what you recommend?+

Yes, commissions vary by carrier and plan, and some options, like staying on your work plan, pay me nothing. That's why I show you the comparison and the reasons, so you can check my thinking.
