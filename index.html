FAQ

What people ask me

If yours isn't here, text me and I'll answer it.

Do I pay you anything?+

No. The carrier sets one premium for a plan, and that number doesn't move whether an agent helped you or not. My commission comes out of the carrier's side. What you add by working with me is the comparison, and a person who answers later.

So the premium isn't higher if I enroll through you?+

Correct. A plan carries one price in your state, and it's the same from the carrier, on the Marketplace, or through me. Agents are paid out of that price, never on top of it. The full breakdown of who pays me.

I'm 1099 and nobody offers me a plan. What are my options?+

That describes most of my clients. You can buy a Marketplace plan, which accepts everyone and may come with a premium tax credit based on your income, or apply for a private underwritten plan. That one asks health questions and can say no, and for healthy applicants it often comes in cheaper with a PPO network. I price both for you.

Am I overpaying for my employer's health insurance?+

Maybe. At most jobs, everyone on the same plan pays the same paycheck deduction whether they're 24 or 60, and adding a spouse often costs a lot more. A plan priced on your age and health can come in lower for some young, healthy people. You'd give up your employer's share and the pre-tax deduction, and a private plan can say no or carve out pre-existing conditions, so I compare the real numbers before you change anything.

How is a private plan different from a Marketplace plan?+

They run on opposite rules. The Marketplace takes every applicant, covers conditions you already have, and discounts the premium by income, but many of its plans are local HMOs tied to an enrollment window. A private underwritten plan screens your health first, so a healthy applicant can get a lower price and a wider PPO network, and it can start most months. The catch is that it can say no or leave a condition out.

Do I have to wait for a sign-up window?+

For the Marketplace, usually yes: Open Enrollment runs from November 1 to mid-January, and outside the window a qualifying life event, such as losing other coverage, is the only way in. A private underwritten plan doesn't follow that calendar. If you pass the health questions, it can usually start the first of next month.
