Health insurance in Odessa and North Tampa: start with the doctors, not the premium

I live and work in Odessa. Half the households I price for moved here from a place with a hospital they loved, and the other half have had the same doctor for twenty years. Both get burned the same way: the plan gets picked before anyone looks at the doctor list.

By Josh Cutlan · independent agent, NPN 22078996
Updated October 22, 2026 · 6-min read
In short

North Tampa has added people faster than almost anywhere in Florida, and the hospitals have followed: three major openings in Wesley Chapel and Land O' Lakes since 2023. Most plans sold here run on networks that decide which of those doors you can use. If you moved here from a state with a hospital you trusted, don't let an HMO hide what's available. If you've seen the same doctor for years, make sure they're on the new plan's list before you switch. Either way, pick the doctors first and the plan second.

What's changed around here since 2020

Pasco County counted 561,891 people in the 2020 Census. The state's 2025 estimate is 648,369, up about 15% in five years and 36% over the decade, with roughly 18,000 net movers from other parts of the country in the last year alone (Census estimates, via the Florida Legislature's economic office and USAFacts). Wesley Chapel, Land O' Lakes, Lutz and Odessa took a big share of that.

Health care followed the rooftops. BayCare opened an 86-bed hospital in Wesley Chapel in March 2023. Orlando Health opened Wiregrass Ranch Hospital in spring 2026 with 102 beds and room to grow to 300, Wesley Chapel's third hospital. Moffitt Cancer Center opened its outpatient center on a 775-acre campus in Land O' Lakes in January 2026, with a proton therapy center behind it. And along SR 54 and SR 56, a new urgent care or specialty office seems to open every few months.

The names are the ones my clients across the area already ask for: AdventHealth, BayCare, now Orlando Health, Moffitt, and Tampa General and USF Health down the road. More doors than ever. Which ones your plan opens is a separate question, and it's the one nobody asks until they're sick.

If you came from up north

You're in good company. In 2023, more people moved to Florida from New York than from any other state, and New Jersey, Pennsylvania, Illinois, Michigan and Ohio all sit in the top dozen (Census state-to-state moves, released October 2024). A lot of you grew up within reach of a Northwestern, a Rush, a Mount Sinai, a Penn, a Cleveland Clinic or a Michigan Medicine, and you're used to picking a specialist and getting in.

Then you got here and learned what every transplant learns: finding a doctor in Florida takes longer than it should. The state's own analysts project Florida will be short nearly 18,000 physicians by 2035, with supply covering about 77% of demand (Florida Trend, reporting the Legislature's OPPAGA study, December 2023). The good news is that the doctors are moving too. My own primary care doctor on SR 54 came down from up north, and the newer practices around Wesley Chapel are full of names that trained somewhere cold.

Here's the catch for a transplant: on an HMO or an EPO, the plan's list is your whole universe. HealthCare.gov puts it simply: you can choose any primary care provider in your plan's network, and the key words are "in your plan's network." If that list is thin, you may never find out that the kind of practice you had back home exists twenty minutes away. A PPO treats the list as a discount rather than a wall: in-network costs less, out-of-network still pays a share. For someone who hasn't found their doctors yet, that room to look is worth something.

If you've had the same doctor for years

The other half of my calls are people who never left. You've seen the same family doctor since your kids were small, and the only question that matters about a new plan is whether that doctor is on it. Three checks before you switch anything:

  1. Search the new plan's directory for the doctor by name and by the office address you use. A doctor can be in network at one location and not another.
  2. Call the office and ask whether they'll accept that exact plan for the coming year, not just the company behind it.
  3. Have me confirm it. Directories lag, and networks change at renewal, so the plan that included your doctor last year may not this year.

If the answer is no, you have a decision: switch plans and keep the doctor, or switch doctors and keep the savings. I'll show you both numbers. I won't make that call for you.

Optionality: what HMO, EPO and PPO mean here

In this area, most Marketplace plans are HMOs or EPOs, and most private plans are PPOs. HealthCare.gov's descriptions: an HMO generally won't cover care outside its network except in an emergency, an EPO covers services only inside its network except in an emergency, and a PPO lets you use providers outside the network at an added cost, with no referral needed. An emergency is covered anywhere, and no plan can charge you more for an out-of-network ER.

What that means on Gunn Highway: an HMO can be the right call when its list already has your doctors and you're staying put. A PPO is the right call if you're still building your list, split time between here and up north, or want a specialist without asking permission. Private PPOs are priced on your age and health, often with wider networks. They ask health questions and can say no, so the Marketplace stays the answer for anyone they don't fit.

What people ask me

Do I need a PPO to use the new hospitals in Wesley Chapel and Land O' Lakes?+

Not always. Each plan has its own list of hospitals and doctors, and a hospital can be on an HMO's list one year and off it the next. A PPO pays something toward any licensed provider, which is the safety net. Tell me which hospital you'd want and I'll check which plans include it before you pick.

I just moved from Illinois or New York. Can I keep my doctor back home?+

A Florida plan's network is here. An HMO or EPO covers nothing outside its network except an emergency, and a PPO pays a smaller share outside its network, so a once-a-year visit up north may or may not be worth it under a PPO. For most people the job is finding the right doctor here, and I can help with the list.

How do I make sure my doctor stays in network if I switch plans?+

Three checks. Look the doctor up in the new plan's directory by name and by the office address you use. Call the office and ask whether they'll take that exact plan for the coming year, not just the company behind it. Then let me confirm it, because directories lag and networks change at renewal.

Is Odessa in Pasco or Hillsborough County?+

Both. The 33556 zip crosses the county line, and that matters because Marketplace plans are priced by county and the plans on offer can differ between Pasco and Hillsborough. Your exact address decides which list you shop from.

What if I'm outside Odessa?+

No problem. In person I cover the north side of Tampa Bay: Odessa, Lutz, Land O' Lakes, Wesley Chapel, Westchase, Carrollwood and the rest of Hillsborough and Pasco. By phone and text I'm licensed in 19 states, including Illinois, Indiana, Ohio and Michigan, where a lot of my neighbors came from.
