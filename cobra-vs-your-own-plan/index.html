Left your job? COBRA vs getting your own health insurance

A couple of weeks after your last day, an envelope arrives quoting a monthly price three or four times what used to come out of your paycheck. You don't have to take it. Here's why the number is so high, how long you have to decide, and when a plan of your own wins.

By Josh Cutlan · independent agent, NPN 22078996
Updated October 21, 2026 · 5-min read
In short

COBRA keeps your old work plan running, usually for up to 18 months, at up to 102% of its full cost, because the employer's share is gone. Two 60-day clocks run at once: one to elect COBRA, one to pick a Marketplace plan after losing job coverage. If you're healthy and not mid-treatment with doctors you must keep, price a Marketplace plan and a private plan before you sign anything. Private plans ask health questions and can say no.

Where the COBRA number comes from

While you worked there, your employer paid most of your premium and you saw only your share on your paystub. COBRA is the same plan with the employer's share removed. The Department of Labor says you can be required to pay the entire premium, up to 102% of the cost to the plan.

So if you paid $120 a paycheck and your employer covered the rest, your COBRA bill can be several times that. Same plan, same card, very different price.

Two deadlines, both 60 days

  • The COBRA clock: 60 days to elect, starting on whichever comes later: the day the job coverage ended, or the day the election notice reached you.
  • The Marketplace clock: a lost job plan is on HealthCare.gov's list of qualifying life events. That gives you 60 days from the loss date to sign up for a Marketplace plan, even if COBRA was offered.

Decide inside those windows. Once they pass, your options shrink to open enrollment or another life event, unless a private plan approves you (it can usually start the first of any month).

When keeping COBRA makes sense

  • You're mid-treatment and the exact same doctors and plan matter more than the price.
  • You've already paid a lot toward this year's deductible and expect more care before December. Switching midyear means that money doesn't carry over.
  • You're between jobs for a month or two and the new job's coverage starts soon.

When your own plan usually wins

For someone healthy in their 20s or 30s, COBRA is usually the priciest way to stay insured:

  • A Marketplace plan can be far cheaper if what you'll earn across the full year, counting the months before you left, earns you a premium tax credit. Losing a job often pulls that estimate down.
  • A private plan is priced on your age and health and can usually start any month. We read what's covered together before you count on it.

A made-up example

A 30-year-old leaves a job on March 31. His paycheck deduction was $95 every two weeks. The COBRA letter says $640 a month. He's starting freelance work and expects about $45,000 for the whole year, counting his pay from January to March.

He has 60 days from losing coverage to pick a Marketplace plan, and at that income he'd likely get a premium tax credit. He's also healthy, so he can price a private plan. Either one could save him hundreds a month compared with COBRA, and he sets an April 1 start so there isn't a single uninsured day.

Careful: quitting COBRA midyear

Some people take COBRA "for now" and plan to switch when they find something cheaper. Be careful. HealthCare.gov is explicit: dropping COBRA voluntarily doesn't count as a qualifying event, so no special enrollment period comes with it. Outside the window you'd sit out until Open Enrollment comes around again (Nov 1 to Jan 15), with coverage starting January 1 at the earliest, unless the COBRA period ends, your old employer stops paying its part, or another life event comes along.

That's why the decision belongs inside your first 60 days, not later.

What people ask me

What's the deadline to sign up for COBRA?+

Your 60 days to elect COBRA start on whichever comes later: the day the job coverage ended, or the day the election notice reached you. The Marketplace runs its own 60-day clock from the day you lost job coverage.

How much does COBRA cost?+

The employer's share disappears, so the bill can be the whole premium plus a 2% administration charge, up to 102% of what the plan costs. For most people that is several times the paycheck deduction they were used to.

Can I keep COBRA for as long as I want?+

Usually up to 18 months. Certain life events can extend it to 36 months.

If I start COBRA, am I locked in?+

Mostly, yes. You can leave it during Open Enrollment, when the COBRA period ends, or if your old employer stops paying its part of it. Quitting COBRA on your own outside the window doesn't count as a qualifying event, so no special enrollment period comes with it, and you may be stuck until the next window.

Does every employer offer COBRA?+

Federal COBRA generally applies to employers with 20 or more employees. Some states have their own continuation rules for smaller employers.
