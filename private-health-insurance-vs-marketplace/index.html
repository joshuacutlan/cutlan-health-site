Private plan or Marketplace: which one costs you less, and why

They both cover doctor visits and hospital stays, but they run on opposite rules. One takes every applicant and discounts by income. The other prices you as one person. Three questions settle which one costs you less.

By Josh Cutlan · independent agent, NPN 22078996
Updated October 21, 2026 · 6-min read
In short

Marketplace plans take every applicant, cover conditions you already have, and discount the premium by income. Private plans price you as one person on your age and health and often come with wider PPO networks, but they don't fit everyone. With a meaningful credit or a health condition, the Marketplace usually wins. Healthy and above the credit's ceiling? Price a private plan.

The Marketplace is built for people the old system shut out

Before the ACA, insurers could reject people over a health history. Marketplace plans ended that. They can't turn you away or raise your price over your health. They must also cover ten essential health benefits, from emergency care to maternity and mental health.

The trade-off is price. Because every applicant gets in at the same rates for their age, a healthy 27-year-old pays a premium set for a pool that includes sicker people. For a lot of people the premium tax credit cancels that out, and a meaningful credit is hard to beat.

Private plans exist for healthy people paying full price

A private plan works the old way on purpose. It prices you as one person instead of a pool, so a healthy applicant often sees a lower number. Many come with PPO networks, so specialists don't need a referral and the doctor list stretches across a wider area.

The trade-off is that not everyone is approved and the coverage list is the plan's own, not the ACA's. Three questions settle it

1. How big is your premium tax credit, if any?

If your household income is well under the 400%-of-poverty line (for one person shopping for 2027, roughly $63,840), you probably get a meaningful premium tax credit on the Marketplace. Private plans can't use it. In that case the Marketplace usually wins, and that's the recommendation you'll get. Over the line, or with a tiny credit, you're comparing full prices and a private plan deserves a look. My income goes up guide covers the cliff.

2. How's your health?

An ongoing condition, regular medication, or a pregnancy in the plans means you stay with coverage that has to take you: the Marketplace or a job-based plan. A private plan is for people in good health.

3. How do you use doctors?

If you travel, work in more than one area, or want specialists without a referral, network type matters. Check the specific doctor list, not only the premium. A cheap plan with none of your doctors isn't cheap.

Two examples with numbers

Both made up. Both are 29 and healthy.

The bartender earns $34,000. On the Marketplace, his premium tax credit covers a big share of a silver plan. A private plan can't touch that, so he stays on the Marketplace and keeps his income estimate current.

The 1099 software contractor earns $95,000. He's well over the cliff, so the Marketplace charges him its full premium, and the plan in his county is an EPO with a local network. A private PPO priced on his age and health is worth comparing. If he's approved and the coverage fits, it may cost less and travel better.

Mistakes I see on both sides

  • Picking a private plan without reading what it covers. Know what's covered before you apply, not after a claim.
  • Walking away from a credit you qualify for. If your income qualifies, don't skip the Marketplace because a friend said private is better.
  • Paying full Marketplace price by default. If you're healthy and over the cliff, at least compare.
  • Cancelling before the new plan starts. Get the new start date confirmed first, so there isn't a single uninsured day.

What people ask me

Is private health insurance real insurance?+

A private major medical plan is real health insurance from a licensed carrier. It's different from short-term plans and health-sharing programs. The key differences from the Marketplace: it doesn't take everyone, and the coverage list is the plan's own, not the ACA's.

Does the premium tax credit apply to a private plan?+

No. Marketplace plans are the only place the credit can be used. That's why the Marketplace usually wins once a meaningful credit is in play.

Who usually comes out ahead with a private plan?+

Healthy people above the credit's ceiling, often self-employed people and young couples paying full price. Price follows age and health, which works in a healthy 25-year-old's favor.

Can I switch from the Marketplace to a private plan mid-year?+

Often, yes. A private plan can usually start the first of any month, so the enrollment calendar doesn't hold you. Get the new start date confirmed before you cancel anything, and remember that leaving the Marketplace mid-year means getting back on usually waits for the next window or a life event.

Do Marketplace plans have PPO networks?+

Some do, but many Marketplace plans are HMOs or EPOs with local networks. Check the network type and look for your doctors on its list before you pick on price.
