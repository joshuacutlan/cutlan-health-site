Open Enrollment 2027: three dates, one deadline, and who can skip it

Open enrollment has three dates, and most people only remember the last one. That's the one that costs you January. Here's the deadline to watch, who it applies to, and who doesn't have to wait.

By Josh Cutlan · independent agent, NPN 22078996
Updated October 21, 2026 · 5-min read
In short

For 2027 plans, the Marketplace window opens on November 1, 2026 and shuts on January 15, 2027. The date that matters most is December 15: sign up by then and your plan is active on New Year's Day. If you're healthy and earn above the credit's ceiling, a private plan ignores all three dates, though it asks health questions and can say no.

The three dates

DateWhat happens
November 1, 2026Open enrollment opens. You can shop and apply for 2027 Marketplace plans.
December 15, 2026Deadline if you want coverage on January 1. Sign up or switch by that day, then pay the first premium by the insurer's due date.
January 15, 2027The window closes. From here on, a Marketplace plan takes a qualifying life event.

These are the HealthCare.gov dates. A state that runs its own marketplace picks its own deadlines, so check yours if you're not on HealthCare.gov.

My advice: treat December 15 as the real deadline. Enrolling after it means a later start date, and a gap in January is a risk you don't need.

Does open enrollment even apply to you?

Open enrollment is a Marketplace rule. Whether it matters to you depends on where your coverage comes from:

  • On a Marketplace plan now, or want one: this is your window. Miss it and the only way in is a qualifying event such as losing other coverage.
  • On a work plan: your employer runs its own enrollment, usually in the fall. That's your chance to drop or change your plan, and the right time to compare private options if you think you're overpaying.
  • Healthy, self-employed, and above the credit's ceiling: a private underwritten plan can usually start the first of any month. Open enrollment season is a fine time to compare, but nothing forces you to wait for it.
  • Turning 26 on a parent's plan: your coverage end date matters more than open enrollment. See my turning 26 guide.

Five things to check before December 15

1. Your 2027 price, not your 2026 price

Plans and prices change every year. If you're on a Marketplace plan, it may renew on its own at a new price, or your plan may change. Log in and look instead of letting it roll over.

2. Your income estimate

Marketplace subsidies are sized to the income you put down for 2027. Self-employed with a bigger year than last? Update the estimate. End the year above your estimate and you repay the extra subsidy at tax time, with no cap starting in 2026. Ask your tax preparer how that lands for you; this isn't tax advice.

3. Whether you'd qualify for help at all

Earn above the credit's ceiling and the Marketplace charges you its full premium. For a healthy person in their 20s or early 30s, that's the moment to price a private plan, which is built around your age and health instead.

4. Your doctors and network

Many Marketplace plans are HMOs or EPOs with local networks. If you travel, work in more than one area, or want to see specialists without referrals, check the network before you pick on price.

5. Your work plan's enrollment window

If you're on a work plan you think is too expensive, find out when your employer's enrollment closes. That's usually your only chance this year to drop it without a life event.

Missed January 15? What's still open

After January 15, a Marketplace plan takes a qualifying life event, such as a new baby, a wedding, a move to a new county, or losing other coverage. Private plans are still an option for many healthy people, since they don't follow the open enrollment calendar. They can decline applicants and may exclude pre-existing conditions, so they're not a fit for everyone.

A simple October-to-December plan

  1. Before November: write down the income you plan on in 2027 and what you pay today.
  2. November 1 to December 15: price Marketplace and private options in one table and pick.
  3. By December 15: enroll so you're covered January 1, then pay your first premium by the insurer's due date.

What people ask me

When does the window for 2027 Marketplace plans open and close?+

HealthCare.gov states open the window on November 1, 2026. Sign up on or before December 15 and your plan is active on New Year's Day. January 15, 2027 closes the window. A state that runs its own marketplace picks its own dates, so check yours.

Is open enrollment the only time I can buy a plan?+

Not always. A Marketplace plan sells only inside the window, or within 60 days of a life event that qualifies. Private underwritten plans ignore that calendar: pass the health questions and it can usually start the first of next month. Employer plans follow your employer's own enrollment period.

Will my Marketplace plan renew automatically?+

Often it does, but your price and the plan itself can change for the new year, and your subsidy may change if your income did. Log in and compare during open enrollment instead of letting it roll over blind.

Is my employer's open enrollment the same thing?+

No. Your employer sets its own enrollment window, often in the fall. That's usually the only time you can drop or change your work plan without a life event, so it's also the time to compare private options.
