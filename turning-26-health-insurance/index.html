Turning 26? How to get your own health insurance without a gap

Most people find out their coverage ended when a pharmacy or a doctor's office tells them. Here's how to know the exact day, how long you have, and how to line up the next plan so there's no gap.

By Josh Cutlan, licensed health insurance agent · NPN 22078996
Updated October 10, 2026
The short answer

Find out the exact day your parent's plan drops you. Aging off counts as a qualifying life event, so you can usually enroll in a Marketplace plan up to 60 days before and 60 days after you lose coverage. Your other options are a plan through your job or a private plan priced on your age and health. Line up the new plan before the old one ends. Marketplace plans start on the 1st of the month after you lose coverage.

Step 1: find your real end date (it's probably not what you think)

"You're covered until 26" sounds simple, but the actual last day depends on the plan:

  • A parent's Marketplace plan: you can stay through December 31 of the year you turn 26.
  • A parent's job-based plan: each plan sets its own rule. Most end during or shortly after the month you turn 26. Some end on your birthday, some at the end of that month, and a few at the end of the year.
  • Some states let young adults stay on longer than 26 in certain plans.

Have your parent check the plan documents or ask their HR team. Write the date down. Everything else hangs on it.

Step 2: the 60-day clock

Turning 26 and losing a parent's plan is a qualifying life event. For the Marketplace, that usually means you can enroll up to 60 days before you lose coverage and up to 60 days after. Miss that window and you may have to wait for open enrollment, which for 2027 coverage opens November 1, 2026.

Here's how a gap happens: your parent's job plan ends on your birthday, July 14. You start shopping in August. Even if you enroll right away, your new plan starts September 1, and you were uninsured for six weeks without realizing it.

Enroll before your coverage ends, not after. A Marketplace plan picked in advance starts the 1st of the month after your old one stops, so a mid-month end date can leave a short gap. Ask about it and plan for it.

A plan through your job

Losing a parent's plan usually lets you join your own employer's plan mid-year, too, but you usually have only 30 days to ask. If your employer pays most of the premium, it's often the better deal. If a lot comes out of your paycheck, compare. Work plans charge a 26-year-old the same as a 60-year-old coworker. My young and overpaying guide walks through the math.

A Marketplace plan

Marketplace plans take everyone and cover pre-existing conditions. If your income qualifies for a premium tax credit, they can be very affordable. If you earn more, you may pay full price, and many plans have local HMO or EPO networks.

A private plan

Private medically underwritten plans price you on your age and health, which often works in a healthy 26-year-old's favor, and many offer PPO networks. They can decline applicants, may exclude pre-existing conditions, and don't have to cover every ACA essential health benefit. I go through exactly what's covered before you apply.

COBRA

You may be offered COBRA to keep your parent's plan. You'd pay the full premium plus a fee, so it's usually the most expensive way to stay covered. It can make sense for a short stretch if you're in the middle of treatment.

The mistakes I see every month

  • Waiting until the coverage is gone. You lose days of coverage and risk missing the 60-day window.
  • Assuming you're covered through the end of the year. That's true on a parent's Marketplace plan, but many job-based plans end sooner.
  • Picking on premium alone. Check the deductible, the out-of-pocket max, and whether your doctors are in network.
  • Going without because you feel fine. Most young people won't need much care. The ones who do usually didn't plan to.

The bottom line

Get the exact end date, then pick your next plan before it hits. If your job pays most of the premium, that's usually it. If not, compare a Marketplace plan and a private plan side by side. Your income and your health decide which one wins. The only real way to lose here is to wait.

Questions I get

When exactly does coverage end when I turn 26?+

It depends on the plan. On a parent's Marketplace plan, you can stay through December 31 of the year you turn 26. Job-based plans set their own rules: some end on your birthday, some at the end of that month, some later. Ask the plan or your parent's HR team.

How long do I have to get new coverage after turning 26?+

Aging off a parent's plan is a qualifying life event for the Marketplace. You can generally enroll up to 60 days before you lose coverage and up to 60 days after. Enrolling before is better, so there's no gap.

Can I just stay uninsured for a while since I'm healthy?+

You can, but one accident or ER visit can cost far more than a year of premiums. And if you miss your 60-day window, you may have to wait for open enrollment to get a Marketplace plan.

Should I take COBRA from my parent's plan?+

You may be offered COBRA to keep the same plan, but you'd pay the full premium plus a fee, which is often expensive. Compare it with your other options first.

Can I get a private plan at 26?+

Yes. Private medically underwritten plans can usually start any month and are priced on your age and health, which often works in a healthy 26-year-old's favor. They can decline applicants and may exclude pre-existing conditions, so compare them with a Marketplace plan.
